Blog & Resources

Practical guides for caring from a distance.

Simple checklists you can use today, written for families looking after parents.

Hospital3 min read

Hospital visit checklist for senior parents

Make every doctor visit calmer and more useful.

Read checklist
  • Keep ID, insurance papers and all previous reports in one folder.
  • Carry a photo or list of current medicines with their doses.
  • Note allergies and past surgeries on one page.
  • Write down questions for the doctor before you go.
  • Ask for a written summary and photograph every prescription and report.
  • Take a companion who can listen, note instructions and share them with family.
Travel4 min read

Travelling with elderly parents by air or rail

Small steps that remove most of the stress.

Read checklist
  • Ask for wheelchair or assistance when booking the ticket.
  • Reach the airport or station early, with ID and a printed or saved ticket.
  • Keep medicines, water and a snack in hand luggage.
  • Share live location and arrival details with family.
  • Confirm who is receiving them at the destination.
  • Choose comfortable footwear and keep luggage light.
Documents3 min read

Documents to keep ready for bank work

Most delays happen because one paper is missing.

Read checklist
  • Photo ID and address proof, plus PAN where needed.
  • Passbook or cheque book and the mobile number linked to the account.
  • Updated nominee details and KYC.
  • Pensioners: keep the annual life certificate in mind.
  • Never share your OTP, PIN or card details with anyone.
Safety3 min read

Protecting parents from phone and banking fraud

A few habits that block most scams.

Read checklist
  • Banks never ask for OTP, PIN or CVV on a call.
  • Do not click links or install apps sent by unknown callers.
  • Call the bank back on the number printed on the card.
  • Set sensible daily transaction limits on the account.
  • Report fraud quickly to your bank and the national cyber crime helpline, 1930.
Wellbeing4 min read

How to talk to parents about accepting help

Independence matters. Start there.

Read checklist
  • Begin with their worries, not your own.
  • Present help as a convenience, not as a sign of weakness.
  • Suggest one trial visit with no commitment.
  • Let them choose the time, place and person where possible.
  • Keep the same companion so trust can build.
Wellbeing3 min read

Temple visits and family functions with elders

Enjoy the day without the exhaustion.

Read checklist
  • Prefer off-peak hours and ask about ramps and seating.
  • Carry water, a cushion and any regular medicines.
  • Plan rest breaks; avoid long standing queues.
  • Keep a companion close for steps, crowds and hot floors.
  • Share photos with family back home as the day goes on.

FAQ

Questions families ask us

Who actually comes to the house?

Someone from our verified care network, with ID and references checked — not a freelancer found that morning. You’ll get their name, photo and number before the first visit.

What happens in a real emergency?

Call us and we will coordinate the next steps, contact you promptly, and — subject to team availability — arrange in-person support or hospital accompaniment. We are not an ambulance, emergency medical provider, or 24×7 guaranteed-response service.

My parents don’t want “outside help”.

Most don’t, at first. That’s why the first visit is a conversation, not a service. Start with a single visit and add more only if they take to the person.

Do you handle their money?

No cash from your parents. Spending happens against your written approval, digitally, with a receipt to you.

Can you take them to Jaipur or Udaipur airport?

Yes. Airport and railway assistance can include intercity travel; the companion’s travel cost is billed at actual against your approval, and we plan the trip around the flight or train time.

Which cities do you cover?

We are starting in Kota and adding cities as our verified network grows. Tell us your parents’ city in the form and we will say honestly whether we are there yet. Both services are same-city only; Medical Care covers up to 20 km from the registered home.

Are you a medical treatment or nursing service?

No. FamilyBandhan provides medical assistance and coordination, not medical treatment or nursing. We can assist with doctor and hospital visits, reports, medicines and approved medical-support tasks; we do not diagnose, prescribe or provide clinical treatment.
